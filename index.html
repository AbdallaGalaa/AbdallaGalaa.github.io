Research program

From persistent brain states to the genetics of neurodevelopment

I combine the genetic speed of fruit flies with the mammalian relevance of mouse models to connect disease-causing variants to the circuits and internal states they disrupt.

  1. Ph.D. · 2020–2025

    Persistent internal states

    Hirano Lab, HKUST

    A brief stressful experience can change behavior long after it ends. In Drosophila, I showed that neuropeptide signaling and the blood–brain barrier together sustain a stress-induced internal state.

    PNAS 2026 · first author

  2. Postdoc · 2026–present

    EBF3 and neurodevelopmental disease

    Chao Lab, Baylor College of Medicine & Texas Children's Hospital

    Variants in the transcription factor EBF3 cause hypotonia, ataxia, and delayed development syndrome (HADDS). I am characterizing Ebf3 mouse models and building complementary approaches in Drosophila through its ortholog knot.

    Mouse models · fly genetics · behavior

  3. Next · independent lab

    Variants, circuits and internal states

    Future directions

    My lab will ask how disease-associated variants reshape the circuits that generate and sustain internal states, using flies for rapid circuit-level tests of patient variants and mice to test those mechanisms in the mammalian brain.

    Fly ↔ mouse · patient variants

Neuropeptide signaling and the blood-brain barrier generate a persistent stress-induced internal state in Drosophila

Brief stress leaves a lasting change in fly behavior. This work identifies neuropeptide signaling acting together with the blood–brain barrier as the system that keeps that state in place.
